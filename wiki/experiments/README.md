# Experiments

Every simulation so far, oldest first. Each page gives the question, setup, the numbers, the findings, and what it changed in the design. Results are from small Python sims (usually 4 to 8 seeds). Treat them as evidence for design choices, not as benchmarks.

Level 1 learns its stream, but still no level 2 |

| 20 | [Fair screens, an adaptive vocabulary and a recency move](20-fair-screens.md) | Oct 17 | Screens that score a learned mixture help at every level: the whole corpus reaches 1.939 bits per byte (order-4 counts 2.107, beaten on 17 of 18 books), and a word-level brain beats order-2 counts by 0.225 bits per word with holds and binds over earlier words. The word-piece level now keeps order-2 context but still no chunk |
| 21 | [Word-like units for the level above](21-word-units.md) | Oct 18 | Given whole words, the word level keeps a chunk and a phrase level spawns, on Austen and on the whole corpus (words: 4.710 bits per word against 4.921 for order-2 counts). Above the first climb the phrase level learns nothing, with 64% of its stream "other" |
| 22 | [Fusing units into codes instead of slots](22-fusion.md) | Oct 18 | Fusion is a superposition of role-rotated parts (no thinning, no XOR; 2,048 bits hold about 8 parts). A phrase level fed fused codes still learns no sequence: what looked like learning is matched by a unigram that forgets. Single-winner cells are slots in disguise; using the overlap likely needs a readout over the code's parts |
| 23 | [A factored phrase readout, tested with counts first](23-factored-readout-gate.md) | Oct 19 | With exact counts, a readout over the previous phrase's parts adds only 0.003 bits per word beyond the words 2 and 3 back, which the word level already reaches; not built |
| 24 | [A story state for the word level](24-story-state.md) | Oct 19 | An edge keyed by salient recent content words: no net gain on the whole corpus (4.705 against 4.702); its contribution fades, and 512 words hide most of what carries |
| 25 | [Keeping the words outside the vocabulary](25-kept-words.md) | Oct 19 | Scored on the exact word, the word level gains 0.30 bits per word on the whole corpus (8.395 → 8.098): a memory of recent words names "other" words, and as story keys they make the story edge work; temporary slots make things worse |
| 26 | [A growing word map and a compressed story code](26-word-map.md) | Oct 19 | A word map that grows and organises by swaps as it reads barely organises, and even offline a line keeps about 7% of strongest associates nearby (9 dimensions: 15%); a compressed story code keeps +0.088 of the story edge's +0.130 bits per word but adds nothing to it, organised or not |
| 27 | [Meaning codes in 2,048 bits](27-meaning-codes.md) | Oct 20 | Words given codes learned online from the company they keep group into topics (associates among the nearest 2% ten times as often as chance, more than any low-dimensional map); meaning improves the compressed story code on the whole corpus, but its readout's gains on new words come from compression, not meaning, and nothing beats the story edge overall |
| 28 | [Wider codes, fast mapping and a working memory](28-width-fast-working-memory.md) | Oct 20 | Wider meaning codes keep improving the compressed readout (+0.091 at 1,024 bits to +0.147 at 14,336, beating the story edge's +0.130); fast-mapping new words to the lit story more than doubles the rare-word gain; together at 8,192 bits they add 0.039 bits per word beyond the story edge |
| 31 | [Reading comprehension with a hippocampal store](31-comprehension.md) | Oct 20 | On bAbI-style stories, a fast one-shot store of clause bindings, recalled by cue through a context-dependent gain, answers 98.4% of questions (engine without it 38.7%, order-2 counts 10.6%); with roles learned from context and gates keyed on the question's form it learns which relation answers which question, 99.2% |
| 32 | [A learned, bitwise hippocampal store, and holographic memory](32-learned-bitwise-store.md) | Oct 21 | With nothing hand-coded and only bits and small integers inside, a store with learned clause boundaries (a word is a boundary when what follows it carries little of what came before) answers 76.6% on stories with held-out phrasings (hand-coded store 98.9%, earlier learned store 53.7%); quantized complex holographic traces store far more per bit, most with sparse writes and few levels, but lose to bit planes on recency |
| 33 | [A learned map: place codes and direction codes](33-learned-map.md) | Oct 21 | Rooms placed as integer phase codes and direction words as learned phase steps build a map from relations stated one at a time: answers every relation only implied by the facts so far (and stated ones, after the first 50 stories), with best guesses improving with each fact; north/south and east/west are learned as opposites from random starts (0.97 of the way by 51 stories, exact by 101) |
| 35 | [Precision, spread and compression: testing the trade-offs](35-axes-of-memory.md) | Oct 21 | Seven pre-registered predictions tested with bit-local simulations: counter width is a time constant (1 bit wins on clean data, about 4 bits on noisy; up-only counters saturate), best active elements ≈ 3 × levels² with no trend in depth across levels, a hash table wins on exact cues but breaks on noisy ones, OR prototypes collapse while counters read at 1 bit lose nothing, a sense should add to its word (shrinkage) rather than mask it, a shifting state gives order for free and bounced reads answer chains of any length given a write time per slot; on the stories none of these helped, because the learned classes merge relations that storage then overwrites |
| 36 | [The forum's changes, in Rust](36-forum-changes.md) | Oct 22 | Integer-only switches on the situation model: learned value kinds, questions that don't write, recency within an entity, function words not used as cues, learning over an entity's live slots, and more proposed routes take the small story set from 70.3% to 85.9–87.1% (3 seeds, top answer, no kind filter), 8x faster; a general query learner (a bitwise W_QK trained by a mistake-driven three-factor rule) with an integer frame-code pooler replaces those hand rules and the float meaning module and reaches 87.9–90.3%; a learned hop policy with a learned stop answers 1 to 5+ hops at 100% with a distractor relation; a 426k-parameter transformer gets 12.8% in one pass and peaks at 40.9% trained on the first half; held open queries (two-hop patterns kept answered while reading, opened and closed by an integer balance paid for contribution) reach 91.4–92.6% with the number open tuning itself (about 5 on average, peak 16, the cap never binding above 16); a read list that takes each kind's newest value first lets the object → holder → place chain through, and with open queries the set reaches 93.6–96.0% (mean 95.1%, about 2.6 open); the full 817k-word benchmark scores 89.5% (seed 0, 71 min on one core; where-object 51.4%); the query weights fit in 2.2 MB at 8 bits (bit-identical to the 23 MB dense table) or 64 KB superposed (−0.5 points); route choice with no weights, the question form as a key in a long-term area of the store learned by mistake-gated Hebbian writes, reaches 86.9–93.0%; curiosity (look it up in the window when memory is unsure, answer if the window agrees, else "unknown") cuts wrong answers from 9.5% to 5.5% with 7.6% unknown |

## Common setup

- **Codes:** 32 of 2048 bits per node output, unless stated.
- **Toy worlds:** 8-symbol nodes, 85% rule-following, top-1 accuracy as the measure. Chance is 0.125.
- **Text:** NLTK Gutenberg *Persuasion* (book A, 300k bytes) then *The Man Who Was Thursday* (book B, 100k bytes), scored prequentially in bits per byte.
- **Replay:** the [Grown Graph Explorer](https://claude.ai/artifact/24hUcx1C3WH2iZFNMFHSfJ) replays the growth runs from experiments 07 to 10 on a timeline.
